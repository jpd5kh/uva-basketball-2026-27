UMES Hawks Scouting Report

Team Snapshot

  • Record: 0–0
  • Conference Record: 0–0
  • Conference: MEAC
  • Head Coach: Malikah Willis
  • Last Season: 20-15 (11-3 MEAC) Lost 2nd Round WNIT
  • Key Returners: Kalise Hill, Jaida Cameron, Jailynn Clayton, Desi Taylor, Kaliya Perry
  • Key Transfers/Newcomers: Makylah Moore, Marley Jeffers, Chasity Wilson, Aliza Murray
  • Style Notes: Physical, aggressive, heavy PnR manipulation, crashes offensive glass

Overview/Identity

UMES plays with an aggressive, physical offensive identity built around creating confusion in wing pick-and-roll, using disguised Spain actions and big ghost screens to force coverage mistakes. Their bigs are heavily involved in the offense, working around ballhandlers from the high post and screening actions to create inside-out opportunities, while perimeter players look to cut, relocate, and attack open space rather than force possessions. They also place a major emphasis on the offensive glass, sending all five players to crash and generate extra possessions, especially from the corners. Overall, they want to create advantages through movement, screening, and physicality rather than relying on one primary scorer.

Three Keys

  1. Guards need to play off two feet, stay patient against heavy help, and reverse the ball.
  2. Be physical against ball screens and hard rolls; keep offensive rebounders out of the paint.
  3. Force bigs to catch outside the paint and keep the ball on the perimeter.

Offense

1. Wing PnR manipulation

What They Do: Use disguised Spain and big ghost action to create confusion in coverages

How They Do It

  • Set wing ball screen with strongside block filled; exchange with roller during ball screen
  • Have variations where big sets backscreen or flares out to perimeter for ball reversal/attack

How I’d Defend It

  • Send tag from corner against roller and work out of rotation (horrible shooting team, primary concern is at the rim)
  • Stay at the level or in drop and keep second big defender low to stunt roll

2. Inside-out offense

What They Do: Play inside-out with bigs working around ballhandlers to create advantages

How They Do It: - Use mostly double big lineups and exchange high-low positioning to facilitate ball reversals and seals - Guards relocate around perimeter and play off bigs to create improvised offense

How I’d Defend It: - Maintain high ball pressure on guards and force them to make quick decisions off the dribble - Deny passes to the bigs and make the guards create for themselves

3. Five-player offensive rebounding

What They Do: Crash offensive glass with all five, especially out of corners

How They Do It: - Stay deep in corners off high PnR and crash glass after drive - Guards use quickness and bigs use physicality to gain advantageous position

How I’d Defend It: - Find man after shot goes up, especially if set in gap help

Defense

1. 1-2-1-1 and 2-3 zone looks

What They Do: Mix in full court 1-2-1-1 and half court 2-3 throughout the game

How They Do It: - Aggressive out of 1-2-1-1 with traps on first dribble across halfcourt and on first inbounds pass - 2-3 aligned with one guard on-ball and the second guard denying high post

How I’d Attack It: - 2-1-2 press break alignment with secondary ballhandler in middle of the floor (turn and go) - Put bigger defender in high post and fill opposite wing to get zone in rotation
