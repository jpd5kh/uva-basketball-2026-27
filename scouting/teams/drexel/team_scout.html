Overview / Identity

Drexel plays with a patient, continuity-based offensive identity built around constant movement, wide spacing, and reads out of away screens, flares, and Horns actions. They repeatedly flow from one action into the next rather than relying on isolated sets, using curls, slips, relocations, and high-low opportunities to manipulate help and create mismatches.

Their 4-out 1-in spacing allows them to attack the paint through seals and post touches while keeping shooters and cutters active around the perimeter. Defensively, they are highly adaptable, mixing a matchup 2-3 with man principles, switching across positions to maintain ball pressure, and varying their help depending on matchups and post threats.

Overall, Drexel wants to stay organized, patient, and difficult to disrupt on both ends by leaning on spacing, versatility, and continuity.

Three Keys

1 Stay connected through flares, away screens, and cuts.

2 Attack switches and matchup zone through the middle.

3 Sprint back, then punish Drexel on the offensive glass.

Away-Screen + Flare Continuity

Drexel uses away screens and flares as the backbone of a motion offense built on constant cutting, paint shows, slips, curls, and relocations.

How They Do It
  • Usually operates from 4-out 1-in spacing.
  • Pass-and-screen-away sequences rotate guards through the perimeter.
  • Bigs seal, screen, and move between the paint and high-post areas.
  • They read overplays with slips, curls, back cuts, and flare releases.
  • Actions frequently flow directly into the next screen rather than ending after the first option.
  • High deny with the screener defender ready to help take away middle cuts.
  • Stay connected through away screens.
  • Go under or stay home on flares when appropriate to prevent easy wraps.
  • Communicate early on slips and late switches.

4-Out 1-In Spacing + Middle Seals

Drexel rotates its bigs in and out of the paint to search for mismatches and create middle seals without sacrificing perimeter spacing.

How They Do It
  • All five players screen, cut, and exchange within the motion.
  • The paint is often cleared before a big fills or seals.
  • Bigs look for high-low opportunities and advantageous matchups.
  • Guards maintain wide spacing to make help decisions difficult.
  • Tag off-ball screens and avoid unnecessary switches.
  • Be physical early and push bigs away from the middle of the paint.
  • Maintain weak-side awareness without overhelping off shooters.
  • Finish possessions with five-player rebounding.

Horns as Primary Set Structure

A large share of Drexel’s half-court set offense and ATO package begins in Horns before flowing into high-low, flare, stagger, shuffle, follow, and ball-screen actions.

How They Do It
  • Two bigs begin around the elbows.
  • Drexel uses simultaneous actions on both sides of the floor.
  • Horns often flows into high-low opportunities or perimeter screening.
  • Empty-corner ball screens and weak-side staggers can develop after the initial action.
  • They are comfortable rejecting the first option and continuing into motion.
  • Maintain gap help without losing off-ball screening assignments.
  • Communicate the first screen before the action begins.
  • Keep the ball out of the paint and take away clean catch-and-shoot threes.
  • Be prepared for high-low entries and slips from the elbows.

Flexible Matchup 2-3

Drexel uses a matchup 2-3 that can look like man defense once players are matched, allowing them to change the offensive picture without completely changing their principles.

How They Do It
  • Begin in a 2-3 shell with defenders matching cutters and perimeter players.
  • Communicate off-ball screens and cuts into established help.
  • Can morph toward man-to-man as the possession develops.
  • The high post and middle can become vulnerable when the zone stretches wide.
  • Overload one side to distort matchup responsibilities.
  • Use spread spacing to isolate favorable one-on-one matchups.
  • Flash and cut through the gut.
  • Move the ball before attacking the closeout.

Switch Everything to Maintain Ball Pressure

Drexel switches aggressively on and off the ball to stay connected, disrupt screening actions, and prevent straight-line advantages.

How They Do It
  • Can switch 1 through 5 against ball screens.
  • Defenders step below the level of screens and communicate exchanges.
  • Off-ball defenders sit low enough to protect slips and cuts.
  • They can jump-switch stack actions and switch more aggressively as games progress.
  • Slip ball screens before contact.
  • Use flares, backside staggers, and away screens to stress communication.
  • Find and attack mismatches created by switches.
  • Play downhill quickly before the defense can reset.

Conservative Help + Late Post Doubles

Drexel generally avoids overcommitting early help, then changes the picture with late doubles after a post player puts the ball down.

How They Do It
  • Help defenders creep toward the post without fully committing on the catch.
  • Doubles can arrive after the ball goes down.
  • Weak-side defenders can temporarily guard two before sprinting back out.
  • They vary when and where the double comes from based on matchup and game context.
  • Be patient on the catch and identify the double before putting the ball down.
  • Keep weak-side spacing intact.
  • Rotate guards around the post to create clean outlets.
  • Attack the defense immediately after the first pass out of the double.
